A sender domain is the domain after `@` in your email's From address. Authenticating that domain allows receiving mail systems to verify that Dugble is authorized to send on its behalf.

For example, sending from `notifications@example.com` requires control of `example.com` or an appropriate sending subdomain such as `mail.example.com`.

## Why domain authentication matters

Domain authentication helps you:

- Establish a consistent sender identity
- Reduce spoofing risk
- Improve inbox placement
- Align SPF and DKIM with your sending infrastructure
- Publish a DMARC policy for the visible From domain

## Add a sender domain

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose a sending domain">
    Use a domain or subdomain you control. A dedicated subdomain such as
    `mail.example.com` can separate transactional mail reputation from other
    mail streams.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add it to Dugble">
    Create the sender domain in your Dugble team and select the provider region
    used for delivery. Dugble currently supports `us-east-1` and `eu-north-1`.
    The first request in a region may report that email infrastructure is still
    being prepared; retry the request shortly. New domains begin in the
    `pending` state.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish the DNS records">
    Add every verification record shown by Dugble at your DNS provider. Copy
    host names and values exactly.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Wait for verification">
    DNS updates may take time to propagate. Do not send from the domain until
    its Dugble status is `verified`.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Domain states

| State      | Meaning                                                          |
| ---------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `pending`  | The domain exists in Dugble but verification is incomplete.      |
| `verified` | The required verification checks have passed.                    |
| `failed`   | Verification failed; inspect the failure reason and DNS records. |
| `disabled` | The domain is not currently available for sending.               |

## DNS guidance

- Enter DNS names exactly as shown, noting whether your provider automatically appends the zone name.
- Do not proxy email authentication records through an HTTP proxy or CDN.
- Avoid creating multiple SPF TXT records at the same host; merge authorized senders into one SPF policy.
- Keep DKIM private keys with the sending provider. DNS contains only public verification material.
- Lowering DNS TTL before a planned change can make later corrections propagate faster.

<Tip>
  Use a dedicated sending subdomain when different systems or teams manage your
  product email and your corporate mailbox infrastructure.
</Tip>
