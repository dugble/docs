---
title: Broadcasts
description: Understand how Dugble sends one email message to a reusable audience.
---

Broadcasts let you send one email template to a segment of contacts while Dugble applies audience eligibility rules before delivery.

A broadcast connects four things:

- a **segment** that defines the starting audience
- an optional **topic** that applies subscription preferences
- a **message template** that provides the email content
- optional **variable bindings** used when rendering the template

## Broadcast lifecycle

A new broadcast starts as a `draft`. Drafts can be edited and previewed before sending.

When you send immediately, Dugble queues the broadcast for fanout. When you choose a future delivery time, the broadcast moves to `scheduled` first and is queued when that time arrives.

Broadcast states include:

`draft` → `scheduled` → `queued` → `sent`

A broadcast can also become `failed` or `canceled` depending on the outcome.

<Note>
Only draft broadcasts can be edited or sent. Only scheduled broadcasts can be canceled.
</Note>

## Audience counts

A broadcast tracks audience and fanout totals such as `audience_count`, `eligible_count`, `suppressed_count`, `queued_count`, and `failed_count`.

These counts help distinguish the size of the selected segment from the number of contacts that were actually eligible to receive the message.

## Templates are versioned at send time

A broadcast references a message template while it is a draft. Before sending, the template must have a published version. Dugble records that published version for the broadcast so the send uses a stable template version.
